Episode 4

The Wicket Gate

Watch on YouTubeSubscribe

The big idea

Jesus welcomes everyone who comes to him, no matter how muddy their story is. We don’t have to clean ourselves up first. We just knock.

From the Bible

“Ask, and it will be given to you; seek, and you will find; knock, and it will be opened to you.”Matthew 7:7 (ESV)
“Whoever comes to me I will never cast out.”John 6:37 (ESV)
“No one will snatch them out of my hand.”John 10:28 (ESV)

What it means

The Wicket Gate
A wicket gate is a small door set in a big wall. It stands for Jesus, the way in (John 10:9). It is narrow, but it is open to anyone who knocks.
Christian’s worries
On the road he remembers every mistake and fears he’ll be turned away. Many people feel this way about coming to God. The gate answers the fear: “We turn no one away who knocks.”
Goodwill
Goodwill is a lion, a picture of Jesus, the Lion of Judah (Revelation 5:5). He already knows Christian’s name and has been waiting for him. When Christian confesses where he has been, Goodwill isn’t angry: the door was made for the bruised and muddy.
Beelzebub’s arrows
Beelzebub is a name for the devil. His arrows show that the enemy tries hardest to stop people right at the moment they come to Jesus. Goodwill pulls Christian in, and no arrow reaches past the wall.
The burden stays on (for now)
Christian hopes to lose his burden at the gate, but Goodwill says, “Not here. Ahead is the place of rescue.” Bunyan is showing that coming to Jesus is the start of the road; at the Cross, Christian will see clearly what Jesus has done for him.

Questions for ages 4–7

  • What did the words above the gate say?
  • How did Christian feel before he knocked? Why?
  • Who opened the gate? What kind of animal was he?
  • What did Goodwill do when the arrows came?

Questions for ages 8–12

  • Christian was afraid his mistakes would keep the gate shut. What did Goodwill say about that?
  • Why do you think the arrows started flying right when Christian reached the gate?
  • Goodwill already knew Christian’s name. What does that tell us about how God knows us?
  • Why didn’t the burden come off at the gate? What do you think will happen at “the place of rescue”?

Do it together

Play “Knock and it will be opened”: one person waits behind a door, the other knocks and says one true thing about themselves, even a mistake. Every single time, the door opens with a big welcome. Talk about how God always opens to us when we come to him.

Pray

Dear Jesus, thank you that your door is open to everyone who knocks. Thank you that you know our names, and that you keep us safe in your hands. Help us come to you just as we are. Amen.
