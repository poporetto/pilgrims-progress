Episode 3

The Mountain of Fire

Watch on YouTubeSubscribe

The big idea

We can’t get rid of our sin by trying harder to be good. God’s rules show us what is wrong, but only God’s grace can take the burden away. When we wander, God calls us back.

From the Bible

“For by grace you have been saved through faith. And this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God, not a result of works, so that no one may boast.”Ephesians 2:8–9 (ESV)
“Through the law comes knowledge of sin.”Romans 3:20 (ESV)
“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.”1 John 1:9 (ESV)

What it means

Mr. Worldly Wiseman
He sounds sensible and kind, and he offers exactly what Christian wants: no mud, a nice house, an easy fix. He stands for advice that makes sense by the world’s standards but leads away from God.
Mr. Legality and Morality
Legality means “keeping the rules”. The idea is that if you are good enough, your burden will go away. Bunyan says no one has ever been set free that way.
Mount Sinai
Sinai is where God gave the Ten Commandments. In the story the mountain shows Christian everything he has done wrong, but it cannot lift the burden. God’s law is good, and it shows us that we need a rescuer.
Evangelist returns
Evangelist is honest about Christian’s mistake, but he doesn’t give up on him. “There is hope.” Turning around and going back to God’s way is what the Bible calls repentance.

Questions for ages 4–7

  • What did Mr. Worldly Wiseman promise Christian?
  • How did Christian feel under the big mountain?
  • What did Evangelist tell Christian to do?
  • When you do something wrong, can you fix it all by yourself? Who can help?

Questions for ages 8–12

  • Why did Mr. Worldly Wiseman’s idea sound so good? What made it wrong?
  • Christian said, “I have not loved perfectly, spoken perfectly, or obeyed perfectly.” Can anyone? Why does that matter?
  • What’s the difference between trying to be good so God will accept you, and being good because God already loves you?
  • Evangelist said the true road was “exactly where you left it”. What does that tell us about coming back to God after we’ve wandered?

Do it together

Draw a simple map with two roads: a pleasant, easy one that ends at a mountain, and a narrow one that leads to a gate. Talk about shortcuts that look easy (blaming someone else, hiding a mistake) and what God’s way looks like instead (telling the truth, saying sorry).

Pray

Dear God, thank you that we don’t have to earn your love. When we take the wrong road, please call us back like Evangelist did, and help us turn around quickly. Thank you for your grace. Amen.
